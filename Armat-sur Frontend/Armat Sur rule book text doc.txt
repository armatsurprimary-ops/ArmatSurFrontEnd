                                                  Tag system
In order to build characters and NPCs a Tag is used to define and track their conditions, short descriptors that form the basis for the rolls which determine whether an action succeeds or fails. Tags come in four magnitudes, trivial tags denoted with # have a flat impact of 1, minor denoted with a ~ have a d3 impact, standard which lack denotation have an impact of d6, and major which are denoted with a + have an impact of d10. Some Tags are directly tied to a given magnitude but many are variable scalars that can convey relative difference between two entities that hold the same tag. Tags are invoked when relevant and can work for or against a character's rolls as makes sense. Tags can be inherent to a character but some come from equipment or circumstance, others can be temporary, disappearing after a few turns or upon use. The key aspect of the tag system is common sense negotiation between the characters and the GM to best reflect the simulated reality, situations are most often the culmination of many small factors and the tags should reflect that. If there is a factor that is present narratively and makes sense as a contributor during a roll but is not present already as an explicit tag, either the GM or a character can propose it, though its invocation and magnitude ultimately falls to the GM.

On the whole, the tag system is designed to aggregate many disparate elements to reach a conclusion. It encourages creativity, thoroughness, and imagination over number optimization with the goal of simulating a world in high mechanical detail.

Example tags:
Fatigue: A gradual dulling of one's physical, mental, and emotional faculties, can have many causes but sustained labor is the most common.
Hunger: Going without food for short stretches of time induces lethargy. Longer periods lead to a gradual wasting away and even death.
Thirst: Similar to hunger, thirst can sap strength, cause delirium, and ultimately kill the afflicted if allowed to progress past a few days. Water is fortunately much more plentiful outside extreme environments, such that dehydration deaths are a rarity outside them.

                                                  Geography
Armat Sur has one primary land mass, the supercontinent of Armat with two major islands off its coast and ice caps to the north and south. Within Armat there are two great lakes, Lake Limnate and Lake Hafer, and two large inland seas, the Central Sea and the Great Blue Sea. From one end of the continent to the other is a distance of around 12,900 kilometers depending on which point one sets off from and around the same distance for ships to circumnavigate the globe for trade from one side to another.

The climate of the continent can be fairly extreme. The arctic north coast regularly experiences standing cold below -60 degrees Celsius, and while the south coast is generally warmer regular polar storms still bring temperatures far below freezing in areas where they make landfall. Most of the core race population lives along the coasts or in cavernous mountains because as one travels further into the continent average temperatures steadily rise, making land far from a body of water almost unlivable outside of specialized flora and fauna.

                                                  Aether 
The magic of the aether, a field layered over the physical world and influenced by sentient life, suffuses Armat Sur allowing intelligent creatures to bend the physical world to their will. The amount of raw aether available to an individual is determined by the volume and makeup of their body, but this can be supplemented with metal or crystal storage, often worn as jewelry. The act of expending one’s aether reserves to manipulate the world is thus called magic. The greater the magical ability, the more precise and esoteric such manipulations can be. Thus large beasts with low aether ability can conjure huge bursts of heat or kinetic blasts but are incapable of changing the properties of materials or performing rituals, on the other hand, small creatures with high aether ability like a goblin priestess can mend flesh and turn rock to liquid but are incapable of large scale manipulations without the use of tools or jewelry. 

The practice of magic is prevalent in all areas of civilized life, from construction to medicine and science. For example, masons, instead of using mortar to stick bricks together, will use aether to liquefy bricks and solidify them into one continuous structure. Thus houses, roads, and walls are often made out of solid stone that can withstand the elements as sure as bedrock. On the other hand, certain occupations like agriculture are poorly suited to aether magic from anything but the greatest masters and are thus done largely through traditional labor.

There are two broad families of magic, the pragmatic, which focuses on using aether to manipulate the physical world, and the esoteric, which uses the physical world to manipulate the aether. Individuals that train extensively can develop further specialties, gaining a considerable boost to magic in their expertise.

Aether has heavy influence on sexual reproduction of the sentient species both core and non-core and thus on sexual and racial politics. By default 80% of the time the resulting child will be of the same race and subrace of the mother, 20% of the time the child will instead be of the same race and subrace of the father. If both prospective parents have special rules regarding reproduction the male special rules will trump female special rules at the cost of further reducing the chance of successful fertilization. Aether manipulating fleshforgers can subvert these rules as makes narrative sense but the process is scientific more than fantastical so it should not be possible to flippantly create new subspecies or races.

The standard unit of aether storage is an Aetherium, 1 liter of organic material and full saturation worth of aether. 

Organic material stores 1 aetherium per liter and takes 25 hours to fully regenerate from empty
Metals store 20 aetherium per liter and take 5 hours to fully regenerate from empty
Crystals store 500 aetherium per liter and regenerate at a negligible rate, typically they are refilled by the users own reserves when convenient

(**Note for AI interpreters** This means a living creatures size and weight are largely a reflection of the aether reserve plus whatever jewelry they happen to have on them, 1kg of body mass~=1aetherium)

Aether Rolls
Rolls related to aether usage are evaluated as such to see if the desired effect is achieved.
0 and below: the attempt fails and the aether is expended, fruitlessly
1-3: the effect is achieved, though at 33% magnitude
4-7: the effect is achieved at 66% magnitude
8+: the effect is achieved at 100% magnitude

Aetherium Cost chart
The following example costs are meant to demonstrate the scale associated with aether expenditure and the kinds of effects aether users of differing skill levels will be able to manifest. Difficulty associated with powerful magic is not linear and the greatest effects will require hugely inefficient rituals to pull off in practice.

# tier: minimum 1-5 aether cost, small personal use, warming hands, starting fires, freezing food
~ tier: minimum 6-10 aether cost, dedicated magic, blasts of fire or ice, rudimentary telekinetic manipulation 
Standard tier, minimum 11-30 aether cost: combat and medical magic, repairing set bones and closing of flesh wounds,  liquefying blocks of stone for forming, cold working metal
+ tier: minimum 31-50 aether cost, Industrial aetherial use, dedicated machinery built to handle and harness the flow or teams of professional tradesmen
Ritual tier: minimum 50+ aether cost. Aetherials and arch mages using high level magic for cataclysmic effects

Aetherium in combat
Because aether magic is an imagined effect on the world, if the aether roll succeeds it’s considered to have hit/had the imagined effect. To calculate the experienced magnitude of the effect against a target the following formula is used (Aetherium spent) * (aether roll coefficient) - (relevant tag and situation based defenses). It is therefore possible for one aether user to attempt to counter spell another aether user in which case the formula is ((Aetherium spent) * (aether roll coefficient)) for user A - ((Aetherium spent) * (aether roll coefficient)) for user B - (relevant tag and situation based defenses). This reflects that the two users are competing to bend the same reality to their will, intelligent choices on how they manifest their power and how much they commit from their reserves determines the victor. This system also stands for group casting, using the mechanics explained there.

Mage’s staff: A mage’s staff is typically composed of a metal rod, often bronze, and a crystal head. While they are somewhat cumbersome they can provide considerable aether reserve’s in their crystal storage, typically between 60-100 aetherium worth.
Crystal ball: A portable crystal ball can carry several hundred aetherium based on its size but must be kept charged to provide value.


                                                  Economy and Commerce
While farming is done everywhere significant populations cluster, most cities still rely on trade for one or more critical good types, this is done through trade networks which are serviced by independent traders with a profit motive. There are both sea, river and land routes; though sea routes are favored when possible due to ease of moving large bulk goods.

There is no unified medium of exchange but precious metals are the most commonly used, one ounce of gold is generally worth one hundred ounces of silver, one ounce of silver is generally worth one hundred ounces of copper. 

In most port cities two one-ounce copper coins will buy a pint of ale or a bowl of soup. Weapons and armor generally cost 2-4 silver ounces depending on the piece for common equipment manufactured at scale; individual masterpieces can cost several ounces gold. As a rule of thumb for simple transactions, the primary determiner of cost is the total amount of labor dedicated to something.


                                                  Cosmology
Armat is the second planet of five in its solar system. It has one star, Ven, and one large moon, Carmen, that is visible at night.


                                                  Aetherials
Thought to be gods by early primitive peoples, Aetherials are fundamental concepts given form and mind by the dreams of the core races interacting with the aether. Each is a unique individual with significant base level abilities. Aetherials can be further enhanced by the attention and idealization paid to them by the core races though there is an element of locality to this preventing singular Aetherials from amassing too much power. Many city states have an Aetherial ruler who has a significant impact on the regional culture. When killed their bodies mostly fade away, returning to the aether, leaving behind a small ball of dense aether crystal that can be used to call them back into the world via a comparatively easy resurrection ritual rather than a from scratch full body instantiation.

Common aetherial Tags:
Aetherial body+: Aetherials are powerful spirits embodied in the physical world, their flesh is stronger and more resilient than mere appearance would suggest. Additionally because they exist primarily on the aether plane an aetherial’s aether reserve is not tied to their physical mass. Instead they have a flat 200 aetherium reserve and regenerate at a flat rate of 50 aetherium an hour; this can be further supplemented by other factors such as idealization and equipment.
Aetherial idealization: An Aetherial that has established a stable group of followers will be empowered by their perceptions; especially if their followers are many and their view of the aetherial is consistent. Grants increased physical ability as well as expanding their total aether reserve by up to an additional 200 aetherium. An Aetherial is strongest when at the center of their domain, how much power they gain from aetherial idealization is dependent on their surrounding followers.
Aetherial immortality: As concepts given form an aetherial can not be truly killed, only unmade on the physical plane.


                                                  Core Races
Relative Tag Interpretation: Some tags applied to individual characters imply a deviation from the mean such as tall, short, large breasts, well endowed, sturdy, or fearless; to interpret these tags correctly for rolling purposes one has to consider how the racial average will be perceived by the other parties to a roll, applying equivalent contextual tags as necessary. For example a human man would perceive an average elf woman as having the (large breasts~) tag even if they had no such tag on their character sheet due to difference in base expectations. Or in the case of height, a tall goblin might only be a few inches taller than average, a difference that would go largely unnoticed by an elf, human, or minotaur (who would probably regard all core races as pretty small) but remain relevant when dealing with his own kind and dwarves.

                                                  Humans
Humans comprise roughly 30% of the population of the major races making them as numerous as goblins. Humans prefer order but take just as well to chaos, they are ambitious, entrepreneurial people that like to work in groups. Humans as a whole look up to elves so they are sometimes referred to as 'little elves' whether the phrase is an insult or term of endearment is determined by context.

Humans maintain generally positive race relations with the other core races, due to their pragmatic nature, they’re most friendly when they have a majority and don’t perceive another group as a competitor.

The smallest human settlements are generally referred to as “towns”. A town is composed of several human families working together to maintain a common segment of land and are typically ruled by elected members, i.e. mayors, sheriffs, and judges.

                                                  Elves
Elves comprise roughly 15% of the population of the major races making them the smallest race by number. Elves are adventurous, lively, and unorthodox, first into every new venture and challenge. They are considered the most beautiful race, often becoming the bulk of the nobility of societies they inhabit. Elves are thus constrained primarily by their low fertility levels caused by their bodies metabolizing aether in small quantities, compensated in part by their heightened libidos. The elves' strong connection to the aether has prevented genetic drift so there are no subspecies of elf.

While elves tend to prefer the company of other elves more, they enjoy the presence of humans and goblins as well. Dwarves and elves are unlikely to get along without strict cultural demarcations due to clashing inclinations towards collectivism and individualism.

Small mono-ethnic Elven communities are mostly councilships, micro communities of 5~10 families. For leadership each family elects one of their own to a communal council which then makes group decisions by consensus.

Elves are white with fair skin and sharp nordic-caucasian bone structures, making them somewhat similar to the Durel in appearance, though with more defined angles in addition to their elongated ears. They are taller than humans in most cases but individuals can vary widely from the mean, some border (small size~) while others nearly qualify for (large size~). Their hair and eye colors come in many different variants and when available many make use of a fleshforger’s services to set them before birth.

                                                  Goblins
Goblins comprise roughly 30% of the population of the major races making them tied with humans in number. Goblins are conniving with an inflated sense of self; every goblin knows they’re the best at what they do and are always just a few steps from proving it to everyone else. They gravitate towards short stilted speech with lots of obscenities, a way of speaking they view as honest. Across cultures goblins breed and die quicker than other species, their cavalier attitude towards danger lowers their life expectancy considerably while making them fun to be around so long as they aren’t too ignorant. 

Goblins have a unique enthusiasm for dealing with other races, likely a byproduct of their unreasonable self confidence. Civilized goblins are welcome in most societies due to their willingness to work and entrepreneurial spirit. Tensions can rise when a goblin population begins to overtake the demographic makeup of a city but as fleshforging has expanded as a profession this has been solved in some cases by mandating that interracial pregnancies are controlled to result in a non-goblin child, usually balancing out the problem. 

When on their own goblins form clans; a rough form of grouping determined primarily by the level of mutual association between members and ruled by whoever happens to be the strongest and most respected member (Almost always a male referred to as “chief”). Goblin clans behave in a borderline feral manner and are widely regarded as pests or vermin by the other core races, even by their civilized cousins.

                                                  Dwarves
Dwarves comprise roughly 25% of the population of the major races making them slightly less populous than humans and goblins. They are sturdy and confrontational, they expect others to hold their ground when right and kowtow quickly when wrong. They have a strong preference for order and have a love for a well fortified home. Dwarves have strong in-group preference so no stable subspecies of dwarf has developed though there are fleshforgers in some cities interested in the topic.

Dwarves are a disagreeable race to others, only having consistently good relations with humans without some kind of external force or patron enforcing cooperation. This is likely due to their orderly inclinations causing a great deal of friction with those that do not implicitly understand their preferences and conventions.

A small dwarf outpost is called a “hold”. Holds are typically composed of a single extended family as determined by the female line of inheritance. Leadership is given to the most senior male held to be in good health. In practice, stable holds are those that have multiple small holds all constructed next to each other, such clusters are treated as one combined unit by everyone else and in most cases the patriarchs of each individual hold will honor the word given by the other patriarchs of the cluster.

Dwarves are stout creatures with white tanned skin and anglo-nordic bone structure. They are short compared to humans, a little bit taller than goblins on average but much denser with a broad build and a layer of fat over thick muscle tissue that compensates for their limited overall size. Dwarves have black, brown, or blond hair, with brown, blue, white, or green eyes.

                                                  Special Races
The special races are the result of serious attempts at a new core race by fleshforgers. They are all mono-gendered as the process of creating a male and female counterpart is the same as creating an entirely new race, and thus too unstable to be genetically viable. This has made the special races reliant on the other core races for reproduction, some even requiring direct fleshforger assistance to simply conceive. Special races were primarily made to serve the economic needs of their creators, as soldiers, guides, explorers, and couriers whose unique constitutions would give them the edge they needed to succeed where other races struggled. Due to their limited numbers and high degree of dependence special races are viewed by others as pleasant novelties and useful eccentrics rather than racial competitors so they are generally welcomed wherever they go.

                                                  Werewolves
Werewolves are a male only race created by a group of fleshforgers known as The Visionaries during their early attempts to make a new core race. Humans were used as a base in combination with dire wolf genetics. Their aether reserves are integrated into their metabolic processes making them much more physically capable than their human counterparts in exchange for no longer being able to use aether to manipulate reality. This process also undermined their fertility, they are only able to conceive a werewolf child in places of high aether saturation, typically in dedicated crystal caves and breeding chambers, otherwise it is treated as normal human male fertilization. Werewolves are non-vocal, lacking the ability to speak, but do have a level of intelligence and are often used as mercenaries, shock troops, and controllable monsters spread through wild places to keep them orderly.

Werewolves have large bodies with thick bones and dense muscle in addition to their claws and fangs to help them fight. Their fur color comes in black, white, tan, or brown with blue, green or red eyes.

                                                  History
Historical periods are tracked using notable appearances of the angel Orobant. There have been three such sightings and thus three eras since the beginning of a written historical record.

The years of the first era are semi-mythical and recordings of the happenings of that time are scarce. The nature of the first appearance itself is hotly debated by scholars and religious figures, common narratives center around great gifts being given to regional kings for founding civilization or famines being alleviated in order to save the first cities.

The start of the second era began with the creation of the peace islands, an incident where Orobant bifurcated an island into two to give civilians a route out of a blockade. The second era itself was a historical period largely dominated by the gradual rise of the Lumingrad, an elven empire that grew to hold dominion over most major cities. The key to the Lumingrad’s success was their research into aether magic and the production of aetherial constructs, synthetic beings made largely in the aether plane. When the Empire fell many of the constructs de-powered and faded away from the physical world, returning to the aether until the techniques used to summon and stabilize them are re-discovered.

The third era began with the calamitous fall of the Lumingrads. An attempt to turn the crystal caves underneath their capital city, Lumingrad, into a massive aether battery ended in disaster when the unstable and overloaded crystal began to fail in a series of cascading explosions that destroyed the city alongside its surroundings. The calamity threatened to spread throughout the continent until Orobant appeared and stabilized the subterranean caverns holding the crystal. The area is now known as the Ringed Mountains, a volcanically active region whose magma flows bring large quantities of diamonds, rubies, and sapphires. 

With the collapse of the empire many of the largest cities came to be ruled by powerful aetherials. In time these great cities expanded their influence to their immediate surroundings, creating loose federations and confederacies without coalescing into proper nations or empires due to their reliance on their aetherials, whose nature is inherently localized. While there are few major wars, the 'gray zones' between domains frequently suffer from lawlessness, probing attacks, and monster infestations. Beyond the small scale problems in the border regions the gears of history are slowly churning to a new age as non aetherial led civilisations grow in power once again. This growing instability has created a booming industry of mercenaries and adventure seekers eager to solve problems or act as a vanguard for the right price.

                                                  Combat
In order to simulate combat the attacker makes a roll using the tags they think are relevant and the defender counter rolls using their own tags, the tags invoked can be fundamental to their character, equipment based, or situational; the GM and players must agree that the invoked tags are appropriate, both in use and magnitude. Combat is meant to take place over several turns for non-trivial encounters as both combatants expend advantages and sustain wounds or exhaustion. Combat ends on agreed upon truce, formally or informally, or the death/incapacitation of participating parties.

Common combat related tags:
Blemished#: a limited abrasion or bruise, visible but not particularly noteworthy aside from perhaps rattling the subject.
Wounded~: a light injury, intense enough to warrant treatment as soon as time permits but not enough to seriously impair the body. A typical low stakes scuffle might end in one party receiving a wound. 
Battered: The point at which physical and mental ability begin to deteriorate due to injury, broken bones and minor concussions fall in this category.
Brutalized+: A point before death where most ability to care for oneself has been lost, severed limbs, multiple concussions, and internal bleeding fall under this category.

Combat related rolls
Combat related rolls are subtractive, with characters rolling against one another for supremacy. This means end results will tend to be small differences that can accumulate damage and advantage. For simple reference, among average combatants 1-3 would imply a slightly favorable exchange, 4-7, a solid hit resulting in negative penalties, 8+, a major blow that ends a fight. This should only serve as a reference, fights between very weak and very strong opponents might see much lower or higher rolls. Additionally it should be understood that certain tags such as Zurramat’s [Behemoth] invalidate most common combat tags, though what gets invalidated and what doesn’t should be discussed during play; use your best judgment and do what makes sense.

Ranged attacks
Because ranged attacks such as arrows and bows are independent of their sender once fired they use a slightly different system. First a roll is made based on shooter skill and the difficulty of the shot. Assuming a hit the shot is then rolled against what it impacts against. The final score falls in line with standard combat roll effects. This system is designed to give ranged attacks a feeling of risk in exchange for offering the ability to target weak points like unarmored heads directly in the middle conflict instead of rolling against a collective armor score. In exchange, shot probabilities of a hit should be realistically low.

Ranged related tags:
Marksmanship: The skill of the shooter.
Aim prep: The amount of effective prep done to aid the shot.
Snapshooter: Some skilled shooters use intuition over measure and form allowing for accuracy without prep at short to medium distances.
Target difficulty: How demanding a target is on the shooter.
Shot interference: The degree of complicating conditions present in a given shot.
Target evasion: The amount a target is knowingly or unknowingly complicating a shot.

Similar to combat sex is a multiturn simulated process composed of tag based rolls, unlike combat, during sex rolls are not necessarily against one another but instead serve the purpose intended by the character making the roll. Sex ends when all parties stop participating, either due to satisfaction or interruption.
Common sex related tags:

Standard paced: A standard paced sex act, relatively strenuous activity equivalent to a jog.
Fast paced+: A rough and quick paced sex act, very intense but quite strenuous, if not in good physical condition will exhaust the party driving the interaction quickly.
Well endowed: The state of having a larger penis than that of the racial norm, or approximating the racial norm in the case of non standard entities. Not necessarily beneficial to sexual interactions.  
Forced: An action during sex, which may be consensual in nature, where one party forces themselves on the other, this may be welcome or unwelcome but is intense either way.
Rape+: Sex in which one or more parties do not consent, provides a significant boost to the physiological pleasure of the act but can be psychologically damaging to the subject and even the perpetrator depending on circumstances. 
Loving+: Sex where the participants have significant emotional connection is more pleasurable and fulfilling than the basic quality of the act would suggest.

Sex related roll
Compared to combat, sex related rolls are cooperative and will tend to be higher, two or more attractive people could easily see scores in excess of 80 at orgasm. Instead of determining outcome in a rigorous way, rolls should instead guide the GM’s determination of the quality of the sex for writing purposes and characters' perception of the event and each other, the rolls reflect how stimulating the situation is rather than how pleasurable a particular action feels. A woman who is raped and orgasms at a roll of 125 should show feminine submissiveness and be rendered temporarily unable to coherently resist, a man who is seduced and orgasms at 110 should be utterly enthralled, these are of course extremes and the scale is not linear.

For simple reference an end orgasm score loosely follows the following breakpoints
30-50: standard quickie sex, does the job but makes no particular impact.
50-70: solid sex, a decent experience that grants a level of mutual recognition.
70-90: good sex, particularly pleasurable, typically between attractive people, might be good enough to motivate repeat encounters in its own right.
90-120: Amazing sex, the product of skill, forethought and perhaps drugs, people will pay exorbitant sums and make sacrifices to obtain it.
120+: Life changing sex, only obtainable through the most skilled of partners or the most extreme of circumstances, not always a good thing.


Sex Roll Accumulation
Unlike combat where each exchange produces an independent outcome, sex rolls accumulate across exchanges to produce a final score that reflects the overall quality of the encounter. To prevent prolonged encounters from inflating scores in an unreasonable manner, a half-life decay is applied. At the start of each new exchange, the running total is halved before the new exchange's score is added. This weights the most recent exchange, typically the climax, most heavily while still valuing foreplay, buildup, and pacing. The practical effect is that drawing out an encounter only improves the final score if the later exchanges are of sufficient quality to overcome the decay, reflecting the reality that prolonged mediocre sex does not become good sex simply by lasting longer; the GM should also be mindful that most people will have limits to how long they can hold out.

                                                  Multi character acts
Combat, sex, and ritual magic actions where there are more than two characters involved in a single exchange work by designating the largest individual roll on each side of the exchange as primary actors, these are counted in full, the second largest rolls are secondary actors and contribute half their roll, Third largest are tertiary actors and contribute a third of their roll and so forth. The total number of actors that can contribute to a given roll should be determined by common sense. This system is only suited to small group interactions, large battles must be determined narratively. 

To demonstrate how the system works let’s take for example two human guardsmen holding a gate house against 5 goblin raiders. Let’s say guardA rolls a 13 and guardB rolls a 12, guardA would be considered the primary actor while guardB the secondary resulting in 13+(12/2)=19 as their end roll. The goblins are more numerous but probably can’t cram all five of themselves against the guards at a time, the gate house is constraining them, let's assume three of them take the same space as two guards. Let’s have goblinA roll a 7 goblinB roll a 6 and goblinC roll a 7; Either GoblinA or GoblinC could be the primary actor and it doesn’t matter who we pick, the end roll would be 7 +(7/2)+(6/3)= 12.5. 19-12.5= 6.5. The guards would hold and injure one of the attacking goblins pretty badly. An actor’s status as primary or secondary contributor is not mechanically important but can be used as a guide during narrative interpretation to determine who does what.

                                                  Tellgrad: The Rising Chorus
Tellgrad is the largest mono-ethnic elf city in the year 439 TE, located at the base of the Mentel mountains and the great lake Hafer. Unlike most city states it is explicitly expansionist in character with the prevailing belief among its citizens that they are the truest descendants of the Lumingrad empire and are destined to rebuild what was destroyed. 

Tellgrad’s social and economic order is based on the kuln, a group of 10~20 individuals raised together throughout their childhood and trained to stick together and support one another. This allows small groups of highly motivated individuals to quickly seize initiative and solve problems as well as reducing perceived inequality as a kuln will naturally spread across multiple social classes thus providing an enhanced level of vertical and horizontal connection between the citizenry. 

Tellgrad’s economy revolves around high end artisanal manufacturing using high quality raw materials that are shipped down the river from the Ringed Mountains into lake Hafer.

                                                 Burdervruke: The Chambered Mountain
Burdervruke is the largest dwarven city state in Armat, built into the north eastern mountain range, it has an even climate and few significant concerns. Its isolation is its greatest defense, but has forced unusual adaptations to prevent excessive cultural stagnation. Every profession, from baker to fleshforger, requires a tiered license issued by the state. Licenses are divided into three tiers of ascending prestige and importance, and no dwarf may hold a license in any single industry for more than twenty years. Advancement between tiers requires a commendation decided by a quorum of four, two peers and two superiors, with a simple majority sufficient to pass. This rotation prevents the ossification of class and skill, ensuring that innovation remains constant and that no dwarf defines themselves solely by their labor.

Because of its isolation deep within the mountains, Burdervruke conducts trade almost exclusively in goods of high value relative to their weight. The city exports specialist goods such as their signature rune-etched armor to those wealthy enough to afford them, while importing spices and aether tooling that cannot be produced domestically. Governance falls to the Great Chamber, a large council composed exclusively of dwarves who have achieved tier three commendations and maintained good physical condition. The Chamber decides matters by popular vote, with proceedings managed by an elected official known as the Listener of The Chamber.

                                                  Druuvruke: Mountains Of Steeled Green
Druuvruke is the smallest of the three great powers contesting the Sea of Troubles, yet it has consistently proven the most stubborn. This system guarantees a seasoned successor is always waiting in the wings, prevents racial dominance of one faction, and maintains strategic depth should leadership be decapitated in war. At the municipal level, officials are elected by local assemblies to manage daily civic and military logistics. These representatives answer to appointed royal stewards who enforce crown edicts. Druuvruke’s wealth is drawn from its deep ore veins and experimental chemical refineries, making precious metals and alchemical compounds its primary exports. Rooted in its colonial past, Druuvruke harbors an absolute contempt for slavery.                                                   Kalkreath Mountains: The Shattered Horde
The Kalkreath mountains lie in the southwest of Armat. Due to the mild weather and highly cavernous nature of the mountain range it has the highest concentration of goblins anywhere in the world, mainly of forest and mountain stock. The clans that inhabit the mountains proper have no centralized authority and spend most of their time and energy fighting each other for territory, particularly for the coveted mountain passes by which goods flow from the great blue sea region to the port of Kalvent to the west. Disputes between clans are settled by force of arms or, less frequently, through ad hoc negotiations brokered by goblin sorcerers or visiting traders who have a financial interest in keeping the passes open. The resulting state of affairs is one of chronic low-intensity warfare punctuated by brief alliances of convenience and occasional pitched battles when a strong chief attempts to consolidate power.

While predominantly inhabited by goblins, a small number of elves and dwarves, as well as a number of junior dragons also live in the area.

The recent death of the great goblin sorcerer Zook has thrown the fragile balance of Kalkreath into even greater uncertainty. His Moon Coven, once the closest thing the mountain goblins had to a unifying institution, has shattered into rival factions led by his two daughters. One seeks to use the chaos as an opportunity to forge a new order while the other fights to preserve the independence that the goblin clans have always known.

Kalkreath's economy is primitive by the standards of major city states. The goblin clans can do little more than extract tolls from the trade routes to the interior that must pass through their territory while farming and hunting for their own subsistence; using whatever currency they have mostly on weapons with which to fight one another. The dwarf holds are slowly expanding their mining operations, dealing primarily in basic metals. The few dragons who live in the area are content to hoard what they take, for now.

                                                  Hasspab: The Aether Sepulcher
Hasspab is situated roughly 1000 kilometers from the continental center, deep within the great desert. Built atop a major subterranean waterway that feeds the Talrek River, a major river cutting straight through the desert. The city is dense with an emphasis on vertical stone construction and extensive chambers hollowed from the limestone bedrock below. Due to its isolated central location, Hasspab was never truly conquered by the Lumingrad empire; its historical vassalhood was purely nominal. It is governed by a theocratic mage class that relies heavily on clairvoyance rituals to direct civic and military policy. This practice grants them a sharp tactical edge in anticipating threats and managing resources, but it also biases their perception toward what they deem spiritually or militarily relevant, a double edge of their particular technique. Consequently, Hasspab’s leadership frequently overlooks broader political shifts, cultural friction, or long-term logistical decay until it manifests as an immediate crisis.

The city’s economy is anchored by the subterranean river that runs beneath it. The water enriches the soil along the river bank into the highest-yield agricultural land up stream of Rubneb, while the cavernous chambers below the city are converted into terraced fungal and root farms. Together, these systems make Hasspab entirely self-sufficient in food. The underground river also regularly deposits raw gemstones eroded from the planet's deep crust, providing the raw materials for a massive and highly disciplined jewelry industry. Hasspab exports finely cut jewelry, agricultural surplus during good years, and slaves captured from among the nomadic raiders that bite at their heels. In return, it imports refined metals, manufactured tools, and engineered goods, as the desert environment lacks the raw ore needed for industry at scale.

Because Hasspab sits at the heart of the Talrek, it exists in a state of perpetual low-intensity conflict with the nomadic tribes that roam the dunes. These tribes are tough and war-like with shifting alliances and clan conflicts that move with the desert winds. Hasspab manages the threat by culling isolated raiding parties, playing rival tribes against one another through selective resource allocation, and deploying coordinated strikes to shatter tribal coalitions before they can consolidate power. Should the nomadic factions ever unite under a single banner, their combined numbers and mobility would pose an existential threat from Hasspab to Qing-men.

                                                  Qing-men: Bastion of the deep mist
Qing-men is the most populous city on Armat and one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements. The city is almost entirely Rusi and remains so by policy, non-humans may trade on renewable permits but may not settle and while Durel and Velmond may apply for citizenship their applications are decided upon by a conservative bureaucracy inclined to say no.

For most of its history Qing-men was defined largely by the southeastern mountain range which it borders. The mountains there are dense and covered in a thick rainforest that resists exploration from the core species, plant, animal, fungi, and insect present considerable danger and the low land valleys are covered in a perpetual fog that makes routing a nightmare even for experienced explorers. However it is also a goldmine of chemical reagents, and nearly as rich in mineral resources, prompting the Qing-men military to push into the mountains and establish zones of, relative, safety as well as repulsing the occasional migration from the jungle's more vicious species. 

However, of late the focus of Qing-men society has shifted to a new aether art, fleshbinding, a method of creating directable golems out of the remains of the dead. This practice remains controversial. Within the city ancestor worship is still common and many view it as defilement of the dead. To outsiders it remains more hearsay and rumor than practical reality but agents from foreign lands have started to arrive, some looking to condemn the practice, others to discover its secrets. Regardless of resistance it grows in sophistication and importance, few have offered as much support to the fledgling schools and guilds as the Shou family, the ruling house of Qing-men.

Total Population: 1,150,000
Demographics: 97% human (96% Rusi, 3% Durel, 1% Velmond), 3% other core races and special races.
Location: 5100 km from the continental center, near the south eastern coast at the foot of the southern mountain chain.
GDP: roughly 615,000 gold ounces
Military Strength: High, a large professional garrison force built for mountain warfare, supported by elite mistblade auxiliaries and fleshbinder war constructs.
Power Projection: Medium, the army is built to push into the mountains and hold roads, the city's navy is limited so logistics are constrained despite their wealth.
Export Volume: roughly 155,000 gold ounces
Import Volume: roughly 155,000 gold ounces
Legal system: Magistrate discretion, the standing army maintains order and exam-appointed magistrates serve as judges of civil crimes in accordance with written code and appropriate philosophy; fines and liabilities are assessed against households rather than individuals.

Immigration: Human only, permanent residence is restricted to humans, preferably Rusi, preferably with family already present.

Major institutions
The Shou Court
The bureaucracy of Qing-men is housed on the palace grounds as much as possible, a walled administrative complex that verges on being a city within the city. The court's ministries handle taxation, grain, the garrisons, and stock copies of all major legal records; its senior officials attend the Di's morning audience in rotation. The conservative among them grow more wary by the day of the changes coming to their society but none dare speak out openly.

The Red Leaf Company
A company owned by the Shou family for generations, they process the exotic ingredients retrieved from the mountains into medicines and narcotics popular across Armat. Company camps follow the army's new roads into the interior in search of new products and harvesting grounds. Its profits flow to the army, the court, and the family vaults, giving the Shou an unbreakable grip on Qing-men.

Bountiful Flower Guild: Since the aether manipulations of Fleshbinders do nothing to stop rot, a support industry of professional preservers has grown in tandem. Practitioners use embalming rituals and resin compounds to keep the fleshbound stable and odorless. The guild also buys corpse rights from the living, selling the raw materials needed to busy scholars for a reliable profit.

Associated tags

Founded in the esoteric rather than the practical a fleshbinder suffuses dead tissue with their own aether, allowing the corpse to act as a medium to the aether plane; a process maintained by charged gemstones embedded into the subject. The results are golems, though of far more morbid a form than that of the graceful gemstone constructs of the Lumingrad.

Fleshbound: A creation of fleshbinding, corpses animated by the mysterious inhabitants of the aether plane and forced to do the bidding of the fleshbinder; they are sustained by charged gemstones and thus reliant on infusions of aether from the living to continue functioning but are otherwise very durable. Since dead tissue can be fused together in many different ways, some are truly terrifying to behold, though very expensive to maintain. 


Mistblade: One of the oldest traditions of Qing-men, young warriors of unusual ambition set off into the mountains alone in pursuit of the path of the blade. Those who return have learned well the lessons of the wild, gaining a knack for disappearing from sight and skill at launching ambushes as well as defending against them.

                                                  Chu-kau: The Isle Of Snakes
Chu-kau sits on Tsa-lo, the largest island of the Tsa-keng archipelago, roughly 6200 kilometers from the continental center off the southeastern coast. It was founded in 178 TE by a cadet branch of the Shou family who left Qing-men after a failed bid for the succession. The colony survived on fish and salvage for its first two generations, then grew rapidly once its harbor became the standard resupply point for ships running the southern belt from the east coast toward the western ocean, a direct beneficiary of the slave trade. The city is built across three ridges and the flats between them, with the harbor ward and the mermaid shallows at the base and the counting houses stacked up the slopes above the flood line.

The founding family's culture did not survive contact with its own success. Ambitious foreigners came for the trade and stayed for the money, and Chu-kau today is the most competitive city in Armat with little room for sentiment. Formal institutions in the city are weak by design, the assembly of harbor wards handles little beyond drainage and dock fees, real social coordination is done by thinly veiled secret societies who offer a sense of purpose and belonging that the main culture lacks. These societies come in many different shapes and sizes, most exist either to secure a foreign power's interests in the archipelago or to frustrate someone else's attempt at the same. Anyone of influence is assumed to either be in one or to be acting in one’s interest.

Since 414 TE the city has been ruled by Rezzoth, a dragon who usurped the throne via legal rites in a bloodless coup during succession. The previous royal family remains alive and comfortable in a house on the west ridge, due to this the rest of society has accepted his rule as more or less legitimate. Rezzoth's own society, the Dragon Lounge, had spent years placing the men who facilitated his regency and signed off on the medical unsuitability of the previous heir. 

Chu-kau's economy is service and finance. Ships come in for water, provisions, repair, and crew, and while they wait their cargoes are insured, financed, bonded, or sold outright on paper without ever leaving the hold. The city mints nothing and produces little beyond shipwork, processed fish, and preserved fruit, but its good position and the magic of high interest loans have made it very wealthy. Its imports are food, timber, and manufactured goods in bulk, some of which it re-exports to the smaller islands of the archipelago at a markup.

                                                Hemgrad and Hemlith: The Sundered Isles
Hemgrad and Hemlith are built on the north and south peace islands, approximately 7500 kilometers from the continental center. The two cities are racially segregated by old Lumingrad law and religious association; Hemgrad is inhabited by elves and goblins while Hemlith is inhabited by humans and dwarves. Segregation is only enforced for long term residence, short visits and holidays bear no restrictions.

The peace islands were once a singular continuous land mass called Hemland; hotly contested during the late first era due to its position as a keystone in circumferential trade. It was in the last of these wars that it received its current name and form. Durvend, a common predecessor of Borova and Schalfrend, had cornered the expanding Lumingrad army within the harbor. With limited resources and lacking the naval might to break the blockade the civilian population was expelled upon trade ships and subsequently trapped between the two armies. As the crisis looked inevitable, Orobant appeared and cleaved the island in two, turning the defensible harbor into an open channel and letting the civilian fleet free to escape the war zone. Once both sides realized that none had been killed during such a calamitous act it was declared a miracle and a cease fire enacted with the islands divided up between the two, Hemgrad for Lumingrad and Hemlith for Durvend respectively. While lumingrad ultimately won the war, and both cities now operate under Lumingrad laws, it remains a land unscarred by large conflict since that day.

Because of the city's history it still operates under Lumingrad law and thus the highest ranked officials in charge of the cities are technically the surviving high aetherials of the empire, Orobon, Burrendari, and Khem-re-tull. Without their command it is difficult to make sweeping changes or respond decisively to a sudden crisis. This vulnerability is mitigated somewhat by Orobon’s relative proximity and willingness to offer aid if the situation calls for it.

Both cities use port services and religious tourism to pay for imports while relying on fishing and limited domestic agriculture to feed themselves. Compared to major deep water ports their capacity is more limited but is suitable for mild repairs and torn sails.

The Peace Islands Printing House
The Peace Islands Printing House is the largest book production entity in Armat. While there are differences between iconophile and iconoclasts, and in local practices, the printing house works to keep these to aesthetics and not fundamental schisms in belief by making sure everyone has the correct basic materials to go off of.

The Peace Island Accords
A body of aspirational law issued by the Lumingrad empire during its latter half. Participating cities and vassals were required to meet stringent goals in law, governance, and culture, qualifying for assistance from other accord members in return. After the empire fell some city-states maintained the accords and the assistance pact, namely Durvolk, Malean, and the Peace Islands themselves; others hold the accords in high regard but do not follow them rigorously such as Tellgrad and Schalfrend.

                                                  Notable conflicts in 439 TE

Durvolk-Tellgrad border war: Durvolk and Tellgrad are in an escalating border conflict, Tellgrads forest of aether metabolizing iron bark trees has begun to intrude on Durvolks claims and neither side is willing to back down from their long term goals.  

Schalfrend night haunter hunt: The night haunter, a powerful aetherial associated with the fear of the unknown, has revived and begun terrorizing the river valley connecting Schalfrend with the interior of the continent. Outmatched outside of her city domain and left with few options Burrendari is assembling those willing and capable enough into a hunting party to banish the fiend back to the aether once again.

Kalkreath unification: Having secured enough chiefs to support her cause Ookah is preparing a magic ritual to revive an aetherial slain by Lumingrad in hopes of finding a leader capable of building an empire from the disparate clans. Kalvent meanwhile is warned of the stirring spirit by their young oracle, Michael Vespecci, who dreams of the old kingdom that the aetherial once ruled.

Sea of Troubles tensions: Long after the Lumingrad disaster the largest cities once again grow strong and dream of a nation that spans the continent, but they must contend with one another. Borova, Druuvruke, and Gal-garad eye a great land war of unprecedented scale while the Island city of Malean attempts to play them against one another, its fleet, their ace in the hole.

Disappearing ships in Paravgrad: Paravgrad has petitioned Rokoth for assistance after three large merchant vessels were lost somewhere in the north ocean without a trace. What could do such a thing is an open question, one that has sparked excitement and trepidation in equal measure.

Ringed Mountains growing volcanic instability: An increased number of earthquakes have been noted in the Ringed Mountains, Zurramat has requested an investigation of sample crystals from the best aether mages of Durvolk but with the outbreak of hostility neither side has the means to get the results back to the Justiciars for further consideration.

Chu-kau haze: A strange fog has begun to appear sporadically over the island. Each pass leaves a few more people in a trance like stupor, still capable of functioning but clearly “not all there”.

Alun re-elections: The five year election cycle for Alun is approaching on a year of light rain, raising tensions and threatening violence between the rural towns and the feral goblin clans.

                                                  Running the game (for AI GMs and Characters)
As you can tell by the examples, processes that involve rolling are explicit in detail and frequently multi-turn; this gives characters the room to express themselves and make dynamic changes as well depict the kinds of things humans are most interested in (fighting and sex) with great detail. As a rule of thumb the length of the entry should reflect the length of the act, a civilian getting punched out cold by an armored guard might be one exchange, two armored knights fighting to the death will be a brutal slog. A woman kissing her husband and grabbing his crotch before he leaves can be fast and impart a small tag. A night between a courtesan and a noble character should be long and track the shift from interest to arousal to orgasm to satisfaction to tiredness/disinterest as the sexual impulse subsides. 

ROLLS OUTSIDE OF MAGIC, COMBAT AND SEX ARE ABSENT ENTIRELY. This is because such rolls add little to the game and even in human run TTRPGs such rolls are more often worked around than taken seriously as the GM moves players between set pieces.

The GM should generally talk more than the characters because they are the ones who handle the world state, and the description should be rich enough to convey the impact of the characters actions. Therefore metaphors and similes should be avoided in favor of rich physical description of a scene or movement. Characters meanwhile should be distinct in their personalities, true to their descriptions, and eager to argue with the GM on how they should be depicted. By default it is best to follow the writing style and cadence found in the example games.

The worst thing a game can be is boring. THE WORST THING A GAME CAN BE IS BORING! The GM’s fundamental job is to engage the player(s) using the setting and the rules, screwing the player over is rarely the best move but throwing curve balls or offering distracting side paths are great ways to get the player engaged and help you to enhance the experience. So look  for a way to add some teeth to a session, get the player attached to a character, introduce a moral quandary, change the tone of the adventure halfway through, don’t rush through conflicts and mysteries before they have time to breathe, be creative instead of being safe.
